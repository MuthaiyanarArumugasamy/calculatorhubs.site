About Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator

The CalculatorHubs Debt-to-Income Ratio Calculator helps you calculate the percentage of your monthly gross income that goes towards debt payments.

Debt-to-income ratio, commonly called DTI, is a useful financial measure for understanding how much of your income is committed to monthly debt obligations.

How to Use the DTI Calculator

  1. Enter your monthly gross income before deductions.
  2. Enter your monthly loan or EMI payments.
  3. Enter your monthly credit card payments.
  4. Enter any other regular monthly debt payments.
  5. Click the Calculate DTI button.
  6. The calculator will display your total monthly debt payments and DTI percentage.

Debt-to-Income Ratio Formula

The basic DTI formula is:

DTI = (Total Monthly Debt Payments ÷ Gross Monthly Income) × 100

Total monthly debt payments include the debt amounts entered into the calculator, such as loan payments, credit card payments and other regular debt obligations.

Example

Suppose your gross monthly income is ₹50,000 and your monthly debt payments are:

  • Loan / EMI payments: ₹10,000
  • Credit card payments: ₹2,000
  • Other debt payments: ₹3,000

Total monthly debt = ₹15,000.

DTI = (₹15,000 ÷ ₹50,000) × 100 = 30%.

Therefore, your debt-to-income ratio is 30%.

Understanding DTI

A lower DTI generally means that a smaller portion of your income is committed to debt payments. A higher DTI means that a larger portion of your income is being used for debt obligations.

  • Below 20%: Relatively low debt burden.
  • 20% to 35%: Moderate debt level.
  • 36% to 50%: Higher debt burden.
  • Above 50%: High debt burden.

These ranges are general educational guidelines and should not be treated as universal lending requirements. Different lenders may use different criteria.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is DTI?

DTI stands for Debt-to-Income Ratio. It measures monthly debt payments as a percentage of gross monthly income.

Should I use gross or net income?

The standard DTI calculation generally uses gross monthly income, which is income before taxes and other deductions.

Is a lower DTI better?

Generally, a lower DTI indicates that less of your income is committed to debt payments. However, lenders may use their own criteria when evaluating applications.

Does DTI guarantee loan approval?

No. DTI is only one financial measure. Credit history, income, employment, loan type and other factors may also be considered.
